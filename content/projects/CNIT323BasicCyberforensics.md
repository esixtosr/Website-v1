A Purdue CIT cyberforensics course where I studied digital evidence, file systems, storage behavior, caching, encryption, and investigation methods through simulated case work.

### What this was

CNIT 32300 introduced the fundamentals of cyberforensics and cyber-crime scene analysis. The course focused on how investigators identify, preserve, interpret, and explain digital evidence while considering legal, regulatory, and professional standards.

### What I worked on

- Studied how storage, caching, file types, metadata, and encryption affect digital investigations.
- Practiced reading data left behind in simulated forensic scenarios.
- Connected technical findings to motive, timeline, behavior, and case reasoning.
- Learned how structured methodology helps investigators avoid assumptions and support conclusions with evidence.

### What I learned

This course helped me think like an investigator. I learned that cyberforensics is not only about finding files, but about explaining what the evidence means, how reliable it is, and how it fits into a broader incident or case narrative.
