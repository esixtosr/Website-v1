A personal hardware security lab where I explored Flipper Zero, ESP32 WiFi Marauder, NFC tagging, Bluetooth behavior, wireless spoofing concepts, and BadUSB-style human interface device risks.

### What this was

This was a controlled exploration of portable security hardware and the kinds of trust assumptions that exist around wireless devices, NFC, Bluetooth, captive portals, and USB human interface devices. I also connected earlier reverse terminal learning to BadUSB concepts in a private lab setting.

### What I worked on

- Experimented with Flipper Zero and ESP32 WiFi Marauder in controlled environments.
- Learned about evil portal concepts, spoofing, Bluetooth behavior, encryption awareness, and NFC tagging.
- Studied how BadUSB-style input can affect endpoint trust and user behavior.
- Connected hardware experimentation back to defensive thinking around policy, awareness, and device control.

### What I learned

This lab made security feel more physical. It helped me understand that cybersecurity is not only network traffic and software; it also includes hardware, wireless signals, user trust, and the everyday devices people plug in or connect to.
